# Geopolitical Analysis Skill (Jiang Method Extended)

## Purpose
Produce comprehensive geopolitical intelligence on any country, bilateral relationship, regional conflict, or strategic theme. Combines deep country profiling (political, economic, cultural, infrastructure) with Professor Jiang's game-theoretic prediction framework.

## When to Invoke
- User says "geopolitical analysis", "analyze [country/region]", "Jiang method", "country profile"
- User asks about power dynamics, grand strategy, geopolitical predictions
- User wants to understand a conflict, alliance, or international relationship
- User asks "what will [country] do next?" or "why is [event] happening?"
- User requests intelligence briefing on a nation or region

## Input Types

1. **Country Analysis** — Full intelligence profile + strategic position of a single nation
2. **Relationship Analysis** — Bilateral or multilateral dynamics between nations
3. **Theme Analysis** — A geopolitical phenomenon (e.g., energy wars, semiconductor control, Arctic race, currency conflicts)

## Research Requirements

This skill requires current information. Use available search skills:
1. **Official strategy documents** (national defense strategy, foreign policy white papers, 5-year plans)
2. **Current military deployments and capabilities**
3. **Economic data** (GDP, trade balances, debt levels, key industries)
4. **Recent political events** (elections, coups, protests, legislation)
5. **Infrastructure data** (ports, bases, pipelines, power grid, telecom)
6. **Diplomatic activity** (treaties, summits, sanctions, institutional memberships)
7. **Cultural/demographic data** (ethnic composition, religious demographics, urbanization)

Use Tavily, Exa, or DDG search skills for current information. Use `r.jina.ai` for reading specific documents/reports.

## PART A: COUNTRY INTELLIGENCE DOSSIER

For each significant player, build a comprehensive profile across 7 dimensions.

---

### A1. Political System (Deep)

**Structure:**
- Government type (parliamentary, presidential, federal, unitary, theocratic, hybrid)
- Head of state vs head of government (who has real power?)
- Legislature (unicameral/bicameral, ruling party/coalition, opposition strength)
- Judiciary (independent or captured? Constitutional court power?)
- Military's role in politics (civilian control or military state? Coup history?)

**Power Centers:**
- Who actually makes decisions? (president, party, military council, oligarchs, clergy, royal family)
- Competing factions within the elite (name them, map their interests)
- Succession mechanism (elections, appointment, hereditary, unclear/contested)
- Deep state / permanent bureaucracy influence

**Political Dynamics:**
- Current leader: tenure, legitimacy source, approval/stability
- Opposition: organized or fragmented? Repressed or active?
- Recent elections / political transitions (last 5 years)
- Polarization level (unified → fractured → civil conflict risk)
- Corruption index / institutional trust level

**Vulnerability Assessment:**
- What breaks this political system? (coup, revolution, succession crisis, electoral crisis, foreign intervention)
- Historical precedents for instability
- Current pressure points

---

### A2. History (Last 200 Years — Weighted Toward Recent)

Analyze in three tiers, with increasing depth toward the present:

**Tier 1 — Deep History (100-200 years ago) — Context Layer**
- Empire/colonial legacy (were they colonizer or colonized? Breakaway state?)
- Formation of current nation state (when, how, by whom)
- Major wars that shaped borders and identity
- Foundational ideology or revolution (if any)
- Relationship to predecessor states/empires

**Tier 2 — Modern History (20-100 years ago) — Pattern Layer**
- Cold War alignment and experience
- Independence / decolonization dynamics
- Major regime changes (coups, revolutions, transitions)
- Defining wars and their outcomes
- Economic model shifts (socialist → market, agrarian → industrial, etc.)
- Territorial changes (gains, losses, partitions, unifications)
- Alliance shifts and betrayals (who abandoned whom)

**Tier 3 — Recent History (Last 20 years) — Active Layer** ⚡ Highest weight
- Wars / military conflicts (who they fought, outcomes, trauma)
- Revolutions / regime changes / contested elections
- Economic crises (hyperinflation, default, sanctions impact)
- Natural disasters / pandemics and response quality
- Territorial changes (gains, losses, occupations)
- Key diplomatic shifts (alliance changes, treaties signed/broken)
- Technology/industry transformations
- Demographic shifts (migration waves, brain drain, urbanization)

**National Trauma & Memory:**
- What event(s) define national identity today?
- What historical grievances drive current policy? (may originate in Tier 1/2)
- "Never again" principles (what they'll fight to prevent)
- Unresolved conflicts / frozen disputes
- Historical debts owed / owed to them (perceived or real)
- Generational differences in historical memory

**Trajectory:**
- Rising, stable, or declining over last 200 years? (long arc)
- Rising, stable, or declining over last 20 years? (current arc)
- Turning points that changed direction
- Current momentum (reforming, stagnating, regressing)
- Does the long arc support or contradict the short arc?

### A3. Infrastructure & Critical Assets

**Military Infrastructure:**
- Military bases (domestic and foreign)
- Naval ports / shipyards
- Air bases / missile defense systems
- Nuclear facilities (weapons and energy)
- Underground / hardened facilities
- Space/satellite capabilities

**Energy Infrastructure:**
- Power generation mix (fossil, nuclear, renewable, hydro)
- Oil/gas fields, refineries, LNG terminals
- Pipelines (domestic and international — where do they go?)
- Energy storage capacity / strategic reserves
- Grid vulnerability (centralized or distributed?)

**Transport & Logistics:**
- Ports (commercial and military — throughput capacity)
- Railways (domestic network + international connections)
- Highways / road network quality
- Airports (international hubs + military-capable airstrips)
- Canals / waterways

**Digital & Communications:**
- Undersea cable connections
- Satellite systems (own vs dependent)
- Internet infrastructure (centralized = censorable, distributed = resilient)
- Cyber capabilities (offensive and defensive)
- Data centers / cloud sovereignty

**Water & Food:**
- Fresh water sources (rivers, aquifers, desalination)
- Agricultural land / food self-sufficiency ratio
- Grain storage capacity
- Fertilizer production or dependency
- Vulnerability to drought/climate

**Financial Infrastructure:**
- Central bank independence / reserves
- Stock exchanges
- Banking system health
- SWIFT/alternative payment system membership
- Gold reserves / sovereign wealth funds

### A4. Cultural & Religious Worldview

**Demographics:**
- Ethnic composition (majority, minorities, diaspora)
- Religious demographics (dominant faith, minorities, secular %)
- Urban vs rural split
- Age distribution (young/growing vs aging)
- Education levels / literacy

**Cultural Identity:**
- National mythology / founding story
- Civilizational self-concept (e.g., "Third Rome", "Middle Kingdom", "City on a Hill")
- Historical self-image (victim, conqueror, liberator, trader, guardian)
- Relationship to land/territory (sacred vs practical)

**Religious/Philosophical Foundations:**
- Dominant religious worldview and its political influence
- Eschatology — what do they believe about the "end state"?
- Is religion used as tool of state or independent force?
- Secularism level (enforced, organic, declining)
- Religious minorities — tolerated, persecuted, or powerful?

**Social Cohesion:**
- Trust levels (interpersonal + institutional)
- Social contract (what do citizens expect from state?)
- Willingness to sacrifice for nation (military service culture)
- Protest culture (docile, periodic, chronic)
- Media landscape (free, captured, fragmented, state-controlled)

**Soft Power:**
- Cultural exports (film, music, food, fashion, language)
- Educational prestige (universities, research)
- Diaspora influence globally
- Brand/reputation internationally
- Tourism as cultural bridge

## Key Analytical Principles

1. **Read official documents** — Governments publish their strategies. Most people just don't read them.
2. **Follow the incentives, not the rhetoric** — What they say ≠ what they must do.
3. **Geography is destiny** — Mountains, seas, and choke points determine more than politics.
4. **Energy = Power** — Whoever controls energy controls food, industry, and people.
5. **No one is really in charge** — Everyone protects their incentives within the system.
6. **Systems are fragile** — Non-cooperation by enough actors collapses any structure.
7. **History rhymes** — Same dynamics replay with different actors.
8. **Empires always fall** — The question is when and how, not if.
9. **Internal divisions kill** — Every system has fracture lines; find them.
10. **Follow the money** — Financial flows reveal true alliances and dependencies.
11. **Culture constrains strategy** — A nation can only act within what its people will accept.
12. **Infrastructure is fate** — You can only project power along your logistics lines.

## Limitations & Caveats

- This is one analytical lens among many (realpolitik/structural). It deprioritizes individual agency, moral forces, random events, and technological surprise.
- Probability assignments are subjective — present as scenarios, not certainties.
- Always note what would falsify the analysis.
- Acknowledge when information is speculative vs documented.
- Country dossiers may be incomplete without classified information — note gaps explicitly.
- Bias check: Jiang's framework has a structural-pessimist lean. Balance with possibility of cooperation, institutions holding, and human creativity solving problems.
