# Due Diligence Ladder

A thesis is a hunting ground, NOT a buy list. Never skip levels.

```
Level 0: "I read a thesis"                    ← DO NOT BUY HERE
Level 1: "I understand the industry"          ← 1-2 weeks of reading
Level 2: "I understand THIS company"          ← earnings calls, financials
Level 3: "I can articulate what kills this"   ← bear case research
Level 4: "I have a price and a reason to sell"← entry/exit defined
Level 5: "I deploy 2-3% of portfolio"         ← small, sized for uncertainty
```

## Level 1: Understand the Industry (Week 1-2)

- Read 3-4 **industry** articles (trade publications, not financial analysis)
- Google: `"[theme] shortage 2026"` or `"[theme] crisis 2026"`
- Goal: Can you explain the demand driver to a friend without notes?

## Level 2: Understand the Company (Week 2-3)

- Listen to last 2 earnings calls (free: investor relations page or Seeking Alpha)
- Read the 10-K MD&A section (Management Discussion & Analysis)
- Run: `market_data.py financials <TICKER>` and `market_data.py price <TICKER>`
- Goal: Do you understand how they make money and what drives growth?

## Level 3: Bear Case (MOST IMPORTANT)

Use the bear case research methodology (see `bear-case-research.md`).

Before ever buying, write down 4 specific scenarios that kill the thesis — one per category (demand, competition, execution, valuation). Then research each.

**If you can't articulate what kills it, you don't understand it well enough to buy it.**

## Level 4: Define Entry & Exit

- "I buy at $X or below because [valuation rationale]"
- "I sell if [specific measurable event] happens"
- "I add more if [thesis strengthening signal] occurs"
- "My time horizon is [X months/years]"

## Level 5: Size Appropriately

| Conviction | Position Size | When |
|-----------|--------------|------|
| High (score 2.5+, bear risk < 30%) | Up to 5% | After completing all levels |
| Medium (score 2.0-2.5, bear risk 30-60%) | 2-3% | After completing all levels |
| Speculative (earlier stage thesis) | 1-2% | Clear catalyst timeline |
| Never | > 10% in a single early bet | — |

Scale in over time as thesis confirms. Never all-in on day 1.

## The "Pick ONE Theme" Rule

You don't need to be right about 5 themes. You need to be right about ONE.

How to pick your theme:
- Which would you genuinely enjoy reading about for weeks?
- Which connects to knowledge you already have?
- Which would you notice signals about in daily life?

Your edge comes from sustained attention, not from picking the "best" theme on paper.

## When the User Asks "Should I Buy X?"

Always assess what level they're at and guide them to the NEXT level — never jump to a buy/sell conclusion. The process IS the edge.
