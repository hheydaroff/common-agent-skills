# Phase Framework

How to assess where an opportunity sits in its lifecycle. Buy in Phase 2 only.

```
Phase 1: Research / patents / early pilots
         → TOO EARLY (no revenue, no timeline, pure speculation)

Phase 2: Contracts signed / capacity allocated / earnings inflecting
         → SWEET SPOT (real commitment, market still skeptical)

Phase 3: Analyst upgrades / ETF inclusion / media hype
         → TOO LATE (already repriced, buying from Phase 2 buyers)
```

## Phase 2 Signals

- Customers signing long-term agreements (not MOUs)
- Supply tightening — inventory drawdowns, lead-time extensions
- Revenue inflecting upward after 2+ years of decline
- < 15 analysts covering, most with "Hold" ratings
- P/E below 15x on depressed earnings (market assumes no recovery)

## Phase Assessment Criteria

| Indicator | Early Phase 2 | Mid Phase 2 | Late Phase 2 | Phase 3 (too late) |
|-----------|--------------|-------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Stock move from lows | < 50% | 50–150% | > 150% | > 200% |
| Analyst coverage | < 5 | 5–10 | 10–15 | 15+ |
| Consensus rating | Sell/Hold | Hold | Hold → Buy shift | Strong Buy |
| Media attention | Zero | Occasional | Growing | Headlines |
| Revenue trend | Flat (inflection) | Growing | Accelerating | Priced in |

## The Bottleneck Cascade (AI-Specific)

Market prices in one constraint at a time, then rotates:
```
2023–2024: GPUs (Nvidia)                    ✓ DONE
2024–2025: Memory/Storage (Seagate, WD)     ✓ DONE
2025–2026: Networking/Interconnects          ← HAPPENING NOW
2026–2028: POWER & ELECTRICAL EQUIPMENT     ← Phase 2
2027–2030: Nuclear / Alternative Energy     ← Phase 1→2
```

Core principle: the deeper in the supply chain, the earlier and more boring = more mispriced.

## European-Specific Phase 2 Themes

```
Defense Ramp (Zeitenwende):
  2022–2024: Orders announced (Rheinmetall, SAAB)     ✓ Phase 3 for leaders
  2025–2027: Supply chain catch-up (munitions, components) ← Phase 2
  2027–2030: Shipbuilding, aerospace delivery          ← Phase 1→2

EU Energy Transition:
  2020–2024: Offshore wind build-out announced         ✓ Phase 3 for OEMs
  2025–2027: Grid infrastructure (cables, transformers) ← Phase 2
  2027–2030: Hydrogen infrastructure (electrolyzers)   ← Phase 1→2

European Reshoring / Strategic Autonomy:
  2024–2025: EU Chips Act, IPCEI funding announced     ✓ Headlines
  2025–2027: Construction & equipment suppliers        ← Phase 2
  2027–2030: Production ramp (specialty chemicals, pharma API) ← Phase 1→2
```

**Key EU commitment signals:**
- EU Commission directives & regulations (binding, not just proposals)
- IPCEI (Important Projects of Common European Interest) funding
- European Investment Bank (EIB) project finance
- National procurement via TED (Tenders Electronic Daily)
- Zeitenwende/defense budget legislation (Germany, Nordics)

## Upstream vs Downstream Analysis

Always analyze trends in BOTH directions:

```
UPSTREAM (supply chain)      ←      THE TREND      →      DOWNSTREAM (enabled industries)
What does it NEED?                                         What does it UNLOCK?
Physical bottlenecks                                       New markets being created
Sole-source suppliers                                      Pilot → production transitions
Constraint = pricing power                                 Adoption = revenue inflection
```

**Upstream plays** = picks-and-shovels (boring, constrained, pricing power)
**Downstream plays** = what the trend enables next (emerging, higher risk, higher reward)

Example — AI as the trend:
- Upstream: GPUs → storage → networking → power → nuclear (supply chain)
- Downstream: AI enables → robotics, autonomous vehicles, drug discovery, agentic software

The upstream plays are earlier in Phase 2 (boring, overlooked).
The downstream plays are often Phase 1→2 (exciting but earlier, less proven).

**Best position:** Find the UPSTREAM bottleneck of a DOWNSTREAM trend. E.g., "robotics is coming" → "who makes the harmonic drives that every robot arm needs?"
